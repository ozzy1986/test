How Much Does a Screen Enclosure Cost in Florida?

October 23, 2026 · 3 min read

Thinking about a pool cage or a screened patio? Before you ask for quotes, it helps to know what shapes the screen enclosure cost Florida homeowners see on an estimate. The price depends on the type of structure, its size, the roof design and the screen you pick. Below you will find starting prices, the main cost factors, permit and timeline details, and what is included in the work.

What Affects the Price?

Every project is different, but these factors move the price the most:

  • Size. A larger pool or patio needs a larger frame and more screen.
  • Roof shape. A simple roof costs less than a taller or more complex design.
  • Screen type. Upgraded screen options cost more than standard screen.
  • Add-ons. Extras such as lighting or a new concrete footer (foundation) add to the total.

Permits and the Florida Building Code

Every county in Florida requires a permit and an inspection for this type of structure. Your enclosure must meet the Florida Building Code and local wind-load rules. Where local codes and the approved drawings call for it, enclosures are engineered for wind loads of 130 mph or more.

When you compare screen enclosure cost, Florida permit rules should be part of the picture. Make sure every quote you get includes engineering and permits.

How Long Does It Take?

Most of the timeline is paperwork. The build itself is short:

  1. Engineering drawings: 1–2 weeks.
  2. Permit review: from 1 week to 6–8 weeks, depending on your county.
  3. Installation: 2–4 days once the permit is approved and materials are ready.

What Is Included and Warranty Coverage

We take care of design, engineering, permits and approval from your HOA (homeowners association), so you do not have to deal with the county or the HOA on your own. Your enclosure is covered by a warranty of up to 10 years on the aluminum structure, 5 years on the screen and 365 days on workmanship.
