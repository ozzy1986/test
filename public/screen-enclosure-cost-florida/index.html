October 23, 2026 · 3 min read

Thinking about a pool cage or a screened patio? Before you ask for quotes, it helps to know what shapes the screen enclosure cost Florida homeowners see on an estimate. The price depends on the type of structure, its size, the roof design and the screen you pick. What Affects the Price?

Every project is different, but these are the main factors:

  • Size. A larger pool or patio needs a larger frame and more screen.
  • Roof shape. A simple roof costs less than a taller or more complex design.
  • Screen type. Upgraded screen options cost more than standard screen.
  • Add-ons. Extras such as lighting or a new concrete footer (foundation) add to the total.

Permits and the Florida Building Code

Every county in Florida requires a permit and an inspection for this type of structure. Your enclosure must meet the Florida Building Code and local wind-load rules. Where local codes and the approved drawings call for it, enclosures are engineered for wind speeds of 130 mph or more.

Engineering and permit fees are part of your screen enclosure cost in Florida, so make sure every quote you get includes them.

How Long Does It Take?

Most of the timeline is paperwork. The build itself is short:

  1. Engineering drawings: 1–2 weeks.
  2. Permit review: from 1 week to 6–8 weeks, depending on your county.
  3. Installation: 2–4 days once the permit is approved and materials are ready.

What’s Included and What the Warranty Covers

We take care of design, engineering, permits and HOA approval, so you do not have to deal with the county or your homeowners association on your own. Your enclosure is covered by a warranty of up to 10 years on the aluminum structure, 5 years on the screen and 365 days on workmanship.
